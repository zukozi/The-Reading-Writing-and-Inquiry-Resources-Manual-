Rhetorical Analysis with the Five Analytical Moves

Move 1: Suspend Judgement

  • Delay judgement as you identify your own biases towards the quickest information we need to know and can gleam about the text. Maybe you do not agree with the author's views or maybe you d[...]
    • You should figure out the following about a text before you move forward with further analytical moves:
      • Author: who are they; what do they care about; who are they affiliated with; are they credible
      • Context: the cultural, temporal, and social situation
      • Exigence: what event sparked the author to write the text
      • Genre: essay; video; podcast; news article; blog; and many more "types of texts"
      • Medium: newspaper; magazine; website; YouTube; Spotify; and many more "ways for how texts are distributed"
      • Modes of Communication: textual; visual; spatial; aural; gestural

Move 2: Define Significant Parts and How They Are Related

  • What do you notice?
    • Rhetorical Choices: these can be formal or contextual choices that the author makes to achieve their overall purpose
    • Evidence: the information the author uses to support their claims and achieve their purpose

Move 3: Push Observations to Implications

  • So what? Based on the Rhetorical Choices and Evidence:
    • Purpose or Message: what the author intends to convey to a particular audience
    • Audience or Intended Audience: the author's specific audience

Move 4: Look for Patterns of Repetition and Contrast and for Anomalies

  • Apply "The Method" to Dig Deeper into the Rhetorical Situation
  • What repeats? What goes with what? (Strands) What is opposed to what? (Binaries) What doesn't fit? (Anomalies)
    • Intended Rhetorical Effect: how the author intends for a rhetorical choice to affect the audience; by using The Method you can connect important rheto[...]

Move 5: Keep Reformulating Questions and Explanations

  • Go back through your moves and each part of the Rhetorical Situation.
